We study causal adjustment with observed pretreatment covariates $X$, a high-dimensional pretreatment proxy $W=(W_1,\ldots,W_J)$, and an unobserved confounder. For one prespecified block $j$, the proposed cross-view construction learns a possibly stochastic, discrete or continuous representation $Z_j=\phi_j(V_j,\epsilon_j)$, with output dimension no larger than that of $V_j=(X,W_{-j})$, without using the held-out block $W_j$, and measures treatment imbalance through the conditional distribution of $(X,W_j)$. We prove an exact bias decomposition and show that exact cross-view balance identifies the population average treatment effect under joint latent exchangeability, held-out-view separation, overlap, and outcome-relevant completeness. When balance is approximate, a uniform outcome-relevant stability condition yields the sensitivity certificate $|\tau_{Z_j}-\tau|\le\Gamma_jD_j(\phi_j)$. A finite-class, finite-strata learning analysis and an honest augmented inverse probability weighted estimator give an end-to-end finite-sample bound that separates representation error, hidden-confounding sensitivity, nuisance error, and evaluation-sample variation. The causal interpretation depends on the stated cross-view structural conditions; the existence of one generic correlated proxy is not sufficient by itself.
