\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the Method Delivers}

For one prespecified block $j$, the canonical method uses $W_{-j}$ to construct an outcome-free representation and uses the held-out block $W_j$ to measure the residual imbalance of $(X,W_j)$ across treatment arms within that representation. Under uniform outcome-relevant stability, the product $\Gamma_jD_j(\phi_j)$ is a sensitivity certificate for hidden-confounding bias. This is stronger than reporting a conditional-independence test alone because it targets a causal error scale, but weaker than point identification when $D_j(\phi_j)>0$ because $\Gamma_j$ is not generally identified from the observed distribution.

\subsection{Testable and Structural Components}

Held-out joint balance, empirical overlap, and minimum stratum mass are observable properties. Joint latent exchangeability, separation of the held-out proxy block, outcome-relevant completeness, and outcome-relevant stability are structural assumptions about the full-data law. Exact balance is therefore an observable population condition that can only be assessed up to sampling error, while its causal interpretation still requires structural assumptions. A large held-out discrepancy can falsify exact balance; a small test statistic cannot prove exact balance, completeness, or stability.

\subsection{Attainability and Realized Bias}

The exact identification theorem is conditional on the existence of a representation $Z_j=\phi_j(V_j)$ that attains exact held-out proxy balance. The theorem does not guarantee such attainability in a restricted representation class. In the approximate regime, reducing $D_j(\phi_j)$ reduces the certified worst-case bias under a common stability budget, but it need not reduce the realized bias when candidate representations have different stability constants or when the structural stability condition fails.

\subsection{Learning and Inference Gaps}

The current explicit finite-sample theorem covers finite candidate libraries with hard finite strata and fixed-fold evaluation. The neural experiments use soft assignments and covariance-weighted surrogate objectives that are not algebraically identical to the canonical MMD aggregation. A neural-class generalization bound, a soft-to-hard guarantee, and valid joint inference after adaptive representation selection remain open. These gaps prevent the manuscript from describing every neural result as a direct verification of the theory, even when its empirical error is lower.

\subsection{Audit Views and Practical Scope}

PROBE uses one prespecified proxy block for held-out evaluation and the remaining high-dimensional measurement for representation learning. This design blocks literal self-copying of the evaluated block. It does not by itself establish the required held-out-block structure, so point identification still needs the structural assumptions in Section~\ref{sec:identification}. The experiments repeat the procedure across several held-out orientations and aggregate their estimates as an empirical extension. The population median result in Corollary~\ref{cor:target-majority-median} justifies aggregation when the target-valued cluster contains strictly more than half of the balance-screened orientations. A strict majority of structurally valid orientations is sufficient, but not necessary; the current finite-sample learning and inference theory remains fixed-$j$. The WSC experiment further shows that an encouraging benchmark gap can coexist with a failed practical-overlap rule. Real-data evidence must therefore report overlap, held-out discrepancies, and benchmark uncertainty without treating any one diagnostic as ground truth.

\subsection{Scope of the Contribution}

The defensible contribution is the combination of a blockwise proxy-supervised representation, an observable conditional discrepancy, an exact identification theorem, a stability-indexed approximate guarantee, and an honest finite-sample estimation analysis. The paper does not claim that one generic correlated proxy point-identifies the ATE, that every smaller discrepancy has smaller realized bias without a common stability budget, or that the current neural optimizer reaches the population optimum. A systematic literature audit and broader external validation are required before any firstness or universality claim.
