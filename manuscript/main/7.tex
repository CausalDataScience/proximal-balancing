\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Single proxy balancing asks what a structured high-dimensional pretreatment proxy can reveal about hidden-confounding bias after learning a low-dimensional adjustment representation. For one prespecified held-out block $j$, the cross-view construction learns $Z_j=\phi_j(X,W_{-j})$ and evaluates treatment balance through $(X,W_j)$. Exact cross-view balance identifies the ATE under the stated structural assumptions, while approximate balance yields the sensitivity certificate $|\tau_{Z_j}-\tau|\le\Gamma_jD_j(\phi_j)$. Empirical discrepancy minimization and honest AIPW estimation give an end-to-end bound that separates learning, hidden-confounding, nuisance, and evaluation errors. The current simulations support the method in exact and approximate designs while exposing limitations from unattainable balance, weak proxy channels, surrogate mismatch, and practical-overlap loss. Neural-class generalization, soft-to-hard transfer, stability calibration, and external validation remain necessary for a submission-ready paper.
