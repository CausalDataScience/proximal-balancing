\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

We call our method \emph{PROxy Blockwise Exclusion} (PROBE). PROBE withholds one prespecified block of a high-dimensional pretreatment proxy, constructs an adjustment representation from the observed covariates and the remaining proxy blocks, and uses the withheld block to evaluate residual treatment imbalance. The term \emph{exclusion} emphasizes that the evaluated proxy block is kept out of the representation input, thereby preventing literal self-copying.

\subsection{Related Works}
\label{sec:related-work}

\textbf{Balancing scores and covariate balancing.} The propensity score is the canonical reduction of observed pretreatment covariates: under treatment ignorability given the measured covariates, conditioning on the treatment probability balances those covariates across treatment arms and identifies causal effects \citep{rosenbaum1983central}. Entropy balancing and the covariate-balancing propensity score instead choose weights or treatment models that target balance directly \citep{hainmueller2012entropy,imai2014covariate}. These methods motivate our optimization target, but their usual causal guarantee still requires the measured covariates to suffice for confounding control. PROBE instead allows a latent confounder and asks what a held-out proxy block can reveal about the bias remaining after reducing the other pretreatment measurements.

\textbf{Causal representation learning.} Representation-learning methods compress high-dimensional covariates while penalizing imbalance between treated and control representations. For example, counterfactual regression bounds counterfactual prediction error using factual prediction error and an integral probability metric between learned treatment-arm representations \citep{shalit2017estimating}. PROBE shares the use of a learned low-dimensional representation and a distributional balance penalty. Its distinguishing operation is blockwise exclusion: one proxy block is held out, the representation is learned from the remaining pretreatment information, and conditional treatment balance is evaluated with the held-out block.

\textbf{Negative controls and proximal causal inference.} Negative-control variables can reveal residual confounding because they should not be causally affected by treatment or should not causally affect the outcome, while remaining associated with hidden common causes \citep{lipsitch2010negative}. Proximal causal inference turns this diagnostic idea into point identification by assigning distinct treatment-inducing and outcome-inducing proxy roles and imposing completeness and bridge conditions \citep{miao2018proxy,tchetgen2024ProximalCausalInference}. Semiparametric proximal theory then develops influence functions and efficient estimators for the resulting identifying functionals \citep{cui2024semiparametric}. PROBE takes a different operational route: the remaining proxy blocks form the input to an adjustment representation, while the held-out block measures residual treatment imbalance. An outcome-relevant completeness condition transfers exact observed balance to the potential-outcome means needed for the population average treatment effect.

\textbf{Single-proxy identification.} Single Proxy Control shows that one negative-control outcome can identify the effect of treatment on the treated under a counterfactual proxy condition and suitable bridge structure \citep{park2024single}. This differs from our population average treatment effect target, our latent-confounder proxy interpretation, and our use of an observable balance discrepancy. The comparison makes clear that ``one proxy'' does not by itself identify a common estimand. Identification depends on what the proxy measures, which conditional independence is imposed, and whether the analysis uses a bridge or an adjustment representation checked by a held-out proxy block.

\textbf{Kernel discrepancies and sensitivity certification.} Maximum mean discrepancy is an integral probability metric that equals zero only for identical distributions when its kernel is characteristic \citep{gretton2012kernel}. PROBE uses conditional treatment-arm discrepancies of the measured covariates and the held-out block as observable magnitudes, not kernel-test $p$-values. Multiplying this discrepancy by an outcome-relevant stability constant yields a bias certificate and a representation-selection guarantee. The individual ingredients have close precedents in balancing, representation learning, kernel testing, and proximal inference. We therefore state the contribution through the precise assumptions, estimand, and guarantees below rather than making an unsupported priority claim.
