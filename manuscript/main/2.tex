\subsection{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

\paragraph{Structural causal models.}
A structural causal model (SCM) consists of endogenous variables, exogenous disturbances, and structural assignments. Writing the endogenous variables as $V_1,\ldots,V_d$, an acyclic SCM has equations
\begin{align}
V_j=f_j(\operatorname{pa}_j,E_j),
\qquad j=1,\ldots,d,
\end{align}
where $\operatorname{pa}_j$ contains the direct causes of $V_j$ and $E_j$ is an exogenous disturbance. The intervention $\operatorname{do}(A=a)$ replaces the structural equation for $A$ by the constant $a$ while leaving the other equations unchanged. The resulting outcome is the potential outcome $Y(a)$. An average causal effect is \emph{identified} when its value is uniquely determined by the observed-data distribution for every SCM in the stated model class \citep{pearl2009causality}.

\paragraph{Proximal causal inference.}
Ordinary adjustment seeks observed covariates that block all noncausal paths from treatment to outcome. Proximal causal inference instead allows an unobserved confounder and uses proxy measurements of it. Standard proximal formulations assign two different proxy roles and posit a bridge equation whose solution translates an observable conditional moment into the target causal functional \citep{miao2018proxy,tchetgen2024ProximalCausalInference}. Completeness or injectivity prevents distinct latent laws from producing the same observable proxy law. These conditions do not follow from the mere existence of a correlated proxy. Our method uses outcome-relevant completeness to interpret exact cross-view balance and a quantitative stability condition when balance is only approximate.

\subsection{Problem Setting}
\label{sec:problem-setting}

We observe independent copies
\begin{align}
O_i=(X_i,W_i,A_i,Y_i),
\qquad i=1,\ldots,n,
\end{align}
where $X$ is a possibly high-dimensional vector of observed pretreatment covariates, $A\in\{0,1\}$ is treatment, and $Y$ is the observed outcome. The observed pretreatment measurement $W$ is divided into prespecified blocks,
\begin{align}
W=(W_1,\ldots,W_J).
\end{align}
Each block may be scalar or vector-valued. A block can represent one repeated measurement, sensor channel, laboratory panel, or prespecified region of a larger measurement. We call $W$ a candidate proxy because some or all blocks may contain information about an unobserved pretreatment variable $U$ that affects treatment selection and the outcome.

For each $a\in\{0,1\}$, let $Y(a)$ denote the outcome that would be observed if treatment were set to $a$. The target is the population average treatment effect
\begin{align}
\tau\triangleq\mathbb E\{Y(1)-Y(0)\}.
\end{align}
We allow $U$ to remain associated with treatment after conditioning on $X$. The research question is whether the block structure of $W$ can support a learned adjustment representation that identifies $\tau$ under exact cross-view balance and controls residual hidden-confounding bias when balance is approximate. The representation and the assumptions that give its balance a causal meaning are introduced in Section~\ref{sec:identification}.
