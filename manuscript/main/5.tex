\textcolor{blue}{We test the theory on data whose causal effect is known. Every simulation in this section shares the causal structure of Figure~\ref{fig:exp-design} and the true effect $\tau=1$. In each of them the hidden confounder $U$ is strong enough to reverse the sign of the naive contrast, and adjustment for the observed covariate $X$ alone leaves the estimate near zero. The proxy has five blocks. Blocks $W_1$, $W_2$, and $W_3$ are noisy measurements of $U$. Block $W_5$ is one more such measurement together with two coordinates that act on their own, one causing treatment without affecting the outcome and one confounding treatment and outcome. Block $W_4$ is a measurement of $U$ shifted by the treatment-causing coordinate of $W_5$. A representation that retains $W_5$ adjusts for both acting coordinates; one that holds $W_5$ out has neither, and that choice does not survive the balance screen. }

\textcolor{blue}{Five simulations carry the section. In Sim-1, the base, each measurement is one number, so the proxy has seven coordinates. Sim-2 lets the treatment effect depend on the hidden confounder, and Sim-3 views the proxy through a nonlinear lens. Sim-4 and Sim-5 keep the base model and render the five measurements as $256$-dimensional vectors or as $96\times96$ images, keeping the two acting coordinates of $W_5$ as numbers, which gives $1{,}282$ or $46{,}082$ coordinates under the same causal structure.}

\textcolor{blue}{Each subsection below pairs one theorem with one figure. For every experiment the seeds, sample sizes, learners, and pass criteria were sealed before its data were generated, and every criterion is reported, including the ones that failed. Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism} shows the algorithm itself, carrying the numbers of the positive-control audit.}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textcolor{blue}{Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-proxy-identification}: A Prespecified Held-Out Block}}{Theorem 1: A Prespecified Held-Out Block}}
\label{sec:exp-thm1}

\textcolor{blue}{Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-proxy-identification} fixes the held-out block before the data are seen, so the first experiment holds out one block and balances it. Block $W_1$ satisfies the assumptions of the theorem, with completeness supplied by Proposition~\ref{prop:orc-sufficient}, while $W_4$ satisfies every other condition, including exact balance. If balancing $W_4$ misses $\tau$, the theorem leaves completeness as the condition that fails, and it fails for a concrete reason: $W_4$ mixes $U$ with the treatment-causing coordinate of $W_5$, so a representation can absorb the treatment information carried by $W_4$ while $U$ still carries some. The prespecified contrast is in the estimates: holding out $W_1$ gives $0.999$, $0.999$ and $1.000$ in Sim-1 to Sim-3 of Figure~\ref{fig:exp-thm1}, while holding out $W_4$ gives $-0.442$, $-0.471$ and $-0.440$. The population of the base model agrees: both balancing representations of $W_1$ target $1.0000$, while those of $W_4$ target $-0.4432$ and $0.9530$. Figure~\ref{fig:exp-thm1} then shows what the whole algorithm returns once the block is not named in advance, in those three settings and in the two richer renderings Sim-4 and Sim-5. Adjustment for $X$, adjustment for $X$ with all of $W$, and a learned summary of every block with no balance check all miss $\tau$; the algorithm lands on it.}

\textcolor{purple}{[Figure~\ref{fig:exp-thm1} now draws the sealed family v2 confirmation: \texttt{results/family\_v2/confirm/} for SCM-1 to SCM-3 and \texttt{results/family\_v2/scm4/confirm/} for SCM-4, $n=24{,}000$, $20$ data sets per setting, script \texttt{code/make\_section5\_v2\_figures.py}; the pixel CNN of two SCM-4 data sets comes from \texttt{confirm\_summary\_v1\_amended.json}. The third row is the pixel CNN, which reads every image and never tests balance. The numbers quoted in this subsection still come from the earlier partition: in the new confirmation holding out $W_1$ gives $1.00$, $0.99$, $0.98$, $1.00$ and holding out $W_4$ gives $0.66$, $0.66$, $0.62$, $0.67$, and the earlier fixed-block panel remains at \texttt{figures/section5/section5-thm1-fixed-block.pdf}.]}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textcolor{blue}{Theorem~\ref{thm:unknown-valid-block-identification}: An Unknown Valid Block}}{Theorem 2: An Unknown Valid Block}}
\label{sec:exp-thm2}

\textcolor{blue}{Theorem~\ref{thm:unknown-valid-block-identification} drops the requirement that the valid block be named in advance, and Corollary~\ref{cor:target-majority-median} replaces its plurality by the median once the valid choices form a strict majority. In the population of Sim-1, $8$ held-out choices can be balanced and $7$ of them target $\tau$, so both aggregates equal $\tau$ there. Figure~\ref{fig:exp-thm2} compares three ways of combining the retained choices in each simulation. The balance screen also retains $W_4$, which pulls the average away from $\tau$ while the plurality and the median stay on it.}

\textcolor{purple}{[Evidence available now: the positive-control audit retains $10$ of the $126$ proxy splits, $7$ of which target $\tau$ and $3$ of which target $0.354$, so the realised plurality margin is $\Delta=0.400$ and the largest agreeing component is the target one in all $1{,}000$ evaluation draws. The average over retained splits is not the returned quantity there, so the average-versus-plurality contrast this subsection promises still needs its own computation, either from the audit or from a rerun of the family confirmation under the corrected partition. Figure~\ref{fig:exp-thm2} now computes that contrast from the sealed confirmations: the average over the retained choices is $0.96$, $0.96$, $0.95$, $0.96$ in the four settings, the median $1.00$, $1.00$, $0.99$, $1.00$, and PROBE $1.01$, $1.00$, $0.99$, $1.00$; $W_5$ was never retained in the $80$ data sets.]}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textcolor{blue}{Theorem~\ref{thm:approximate-proxy-bias-bound}: Approximate Balance}}{Theorem 3: Approximate Balance}}
\label{sec:exp-thm3}

\textcolor{blue}{Theorem~\ref{thm:approximate-proxy-bias-bound} replaces exact balance by approximate balance. Proposition~\ref{prop:zero-discrepancy} ties zero residual discrepancy to exact balance, and the theorem bounds the bias by $\Gamma_j(\phi)D_{\mathrm{res},j}(\phi)/\{\eta(1-\eta)\}$ under Assumption~\ref{ass:uniform-stability}, which $W_4$ violates. Figure~\ref{fig:exp-thm3} traces the bias against the residual discrepancy over the representation family in the population of Sim-1, with no fitting anywhere in the curve. For clean held-out blocks the bias shrinks with the discrepancy as the bound requires, while the contaminated block keeps a bias of $1.44$ even where its discrepancy reaches zero.}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textcolor{blue}{Theorems~\ref{cor:representation-causal} and~\ref{thm:conditional-aipw-error}: Learned Representations}}{Theorems 4 and 5: Learned Representations}}
\label{sec:exp-thm45}

\textcolor{blue}{Theorems~\ref{cor:representation-causal} and~\ref{thm:conditional-aipw-error} cover representations that are learned rather than given. The first bounds the gap $|\tau_{\widehat Z_j}-\tau|$ left by the learned representation, the second bounds the gap $|\widehat\tau_j^{\mathrm{AIPW}}-\tau_{\widehat Z_j}|$ left by estimation, and together they bound the error of the reported estimate. Figure~\ref{fig:exp-thm45} reports both gaps at $n=12{,}000$ in the three renderings of the same proxy, seven numbers, $1{,}282$-dimensional vectors, and five images, where adjustment for $X$ and adjustment for the raw proxy both fail.}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textcolor{blue}{Theorem~\ref{thm:probe-search-error}: The Search Budget}}{Theorem 6: The Search Budget}}
\label{sec:exp-thm6}

\begin{wrapfigure}{r}{0.40\linewidth}
\vspace{-1em}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../figures/section5/section5-v2-budget.pdf}
\caption{\textcolor{blue}{Guarantee and observed success against the budget.}}
\label{fig:exp-thm6}

\textcolor{blue}{Theorem~\ref{thm:probe-search-error} bounds the failure probability of the search by $\delta+P(R=0)+L\,\mathbb E\{e^{-R\Delta^2/2}\}$, and Corollary~\ref{cor:budget-rule} turns a target failure level into a budget $m$. Figure~\ref{fig:exp-thm6} uses an exactly solvable control with $2{,}000$ repetitions and $\rho=0.20$, where the bound can be evaluated and the search can be repeated cheaply. The observed probability that PROBE lands within $\rho$ of $\tau$ stays above the bound at every budget, and both rise with $m$.}

\textcolor{purple}{[Data: \texttt{results/e11\_v2\_mcurve\_v1.json} for the budget curve, and the positive-control audit for the guarantee itself. In the audit every split is drawn, so $R=N$ and the bound closes to $1-\delta-Le^{-N\Delta^2/2}$. With $N=10$, $\Delta=0.400$ and $L=1$ the bound is $0.5007$, the three conditions of the theorem hold on the realised retained set in every learning state, and the observed success rate is $1.000$ over $1{,}000$ draws. The separation condition holds with little room: $4\rho=0.6454$ against $g_{\min}=0.6464$.]}
