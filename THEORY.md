**Status.** This document is the canonical theory source for the final cross-view and cross-mask method. It replaces the earlier theory for a representation of $X$ alone. Every established result below is conditional on the stated full-data assumptions. The observed balance condition is testable in principle, but the proxy validity, audit independence, outcome-null-space, and stability conditions are structural and are not generally testable from one observed distribution.

## 1. Goal and cross-view construction

We observe i.i.d. copies of

$$
O=(X,W,A,Y),
$$

where $A\in\{0,1\}$ is treatment, $Y$ is the observed outcome, $X$ is a vector of pretreatment covariates, and $W$ is a pretreatment measurement related to an unobserved variable $U$. The target is the average treatment effect

$$
\tau\triangleq \mathbb E\{Y(1)-Y(0)\}.
$$

The central construction separates the information used to build a representation from the information used to audit it. Write

$$
W=(W^{\mathrm{rep}},W^{\mathrm{audit}})
$$

and define a finite representation

$$
R\triangleq\phi(X,W^{\mathrm{rep}})\in[M],
\qquad [M]\triangleq\{1,\ldots,M\}.
$$

The held-out audit variable is

$$
V\triangleq(X,W^{\mathrm{audit}}).
$$

Thus $W^{\mathrm{audit}}$ is not an input to $\phi$. This exclusion is the feature that prevents the balance criterion from becoming true merely because the representation copied the variable being audited.

For a fixed representation, define its adjustment functional

$$
\tau_R
\triangleq
\sum_{r=1}^M p_r
\left[
\mathbb E(Y\mid A=1,R=r)
-
\mathbb E(Y\mid A=0,R=r)
\right],
\qquad
p_r\triangleq P(R=r).
$$

The theory has two endpoints. Exact audit balance yields point identification under an outcome-relevant null-space condition. Approximate audit balance yields a sensitivity certificate under a quantitative stability condition.

## 2. Assumptions

All variables take values in standard Borel spaces, so the required regular conditional distributions exist.

### CV1. Consistency and integrability

For every unit,

$$
Y=Y(A),
$$

and $\mathbb E|Y(a)|<\infty$ for $a\in\{0,1\}$.

### CV2. Joint representation-safe exchangeability

For each $a\in\{0,1\}$,

$$
A\perp\!\!\!\perp
\bigl(Y(a),W^{\mathrm{rep}}\bigr)
\mid(X,U).
$$

This assumption is stronger than the marginal statement $Y(a)\perp\!\!\!\perp A\mid(X,U)$. The joint statement is needed because $R$ is a function of $W^{\mathrm{rep}}$. It prevents conditioning on the learned representation from creating a new association between $A$ and $Y(a)$ after $(X,U)$ is fixed.

### CV3. Multi-view audit independence

$$
W^{\mathrm{audit}}
\perp\!\!\!\perp
\bigl(A,W^{\mathrm{rep}}\bigr)
\mid(X,U).
$$

This is the cross-view proxy condition. It says that, after $(X,U)$ is fixed, the held-out audit measurement has a common law across treatment arms and is conditionally independent of the representation view. It allows the audit law in both treatment arms to be generated by one common channel.

### CV4. Support, overlap, and kernel regularity

Only strata with $p_r>0$ enter displayed sums. There are constants $p_{\min}>0$ and $\eta\in(0,1/2)$ such that every retained stratum satisfies

$$
p_r\ge p_{\min},
\qquad
\eta\le \pi_r\le1-\eta,
\qquad
\pi_r\triangleq P(A=1\mid R=r).
$$

Let $k$ be a bounded measurable characteristic kernel on the space of $V$, let $\mathcal H_k$ be its reproducing kernel Hilbert space, and suppose

$$
\sup_v k(v,v)\le\kappa^2.
$$

All kernel mean embeddings used below are assumed to exist. Boundedness makes every displayed maximum mean discrepancy finite. Characteristicness makes equality of kernel means equivalent to equality of the corresponding probability laws.

## 3. Conditioning safety and the exact bias identity

The first lemma records why the joint forms of CV2 and CV3 are load-bearing.

### Lemma 1. Conditioning safety

Under CV2 and the construction $R=\phi(X,W^{\mathrm{rep}})$,

$$
Y(a)\perp\!\!\!\perp A\mid(X,U,R)
$$

for each $a$. Under CV3,

$$
W^{\mathrm{audit}}
\perp\!\!\!\perp(A,R)
\mid(X,U),
$$

and consequently

$$
P(W^{\mathrm{audit}}\in B\mid X,U,A,R)
=
P(W^{\mathrm{audit}}\in B\mid X,U)
$$

almost surely for every measurable $B$.

**Proof.** By closure of conditional independence under measurable transformations, CV2 and the measurability of $R$ imply

$$
A\perp\!\!\!\perp\bigl(Y(a),R\bigr)\mid(X,U).
$$

Weak union then gives $Y(a)\perp\!\!\!\perp A\mid(X,U,R)$. This argument is a statement about regular conditional laws and does not require $W^{\mathrm{rep}}$ to be discrete. Applying the same measurable-function closure to CV3 gives $W^{\mathrm{audit}}\perp\!\!\!\perp(A,R)\mid(X,U)$, from which the displayed conditional-law equality follows. $\square$

For a retained stratum $r$, define the treatment-arm laws of the latent adjustment variables by

$$
Q_{a,r}
\triangleq
\mathcal L(X,U\mid A=a,R=r),
$$

and define their signed contrast

$$
\nu_r\triangleq Q_{1,r}-Q_{0,r}.
$$

The outcome regression that is relevant after representation conditioning is

$$
\mu_{a,r}(x,u)
\triangleq
\mathbb E\{Y(a)\mid X=x,U=u,R=r\}.
$$

The subscript $r$ cannot generally be removed because $R$ contains information from $W^{\mathrm{rep}}$.

### Theorem 1. Exact $R$-conditional bias decomposition

Under CV1, CV2, and CV4,

$$
\boxed{
\tau_R-\tau
=
\sum_{r=1}^M p_r
\left[
(1-\pi_r)\int\mu_{1,r}\,d\nu_r
+
\pi_r\int\mu_{0,r}\,d\nu_r
\right].
}
$$

**Proof.** Lemma 1 and consistency give

$$
\mathbb E(Y\mid A=a,R=r)
=
\int\mu_{a,r}\,dQ_{a,r}.
$$

The same conditional exchangeability gives

$$
\mathbb E\{Y(a)\mid R=r\}
=
\pi_r\int\mu_{a,r}\,dQ_{1,r}
+
(1-\pi_r)\int\mu_{a,r}\,dQ_{0,r}.
$$

Subtract the second identity for $a=0$ from the identity for $a=1$, average over $r$, and collect the coefficients of $Q_{1,r}-Q_{0,r}$. $\square$

Theorem 1 isolates the only latent quantities that can bias adjustment by $R$. The next step maps these latent contrasts to a discrepancy involving only observed audit variables.

## 4. Audit channel and observable discrepancy

For a measurable set $B$ in the space of $V=(X,W^{\mathrm{audit}})$, define the audit channel

$$
K(B\mid x,u)
\triangleq
P\{(X,W^{\mathrm{audit}})\in B\mid X=x,U=u\}.
$$

The associated linear mixture operator maps a finite signed measure $\nu$ on $(X,U)$ to

$$
(\mathcal K\nu)(B)
\triangleq
\int K(B\mid x,u)\,d\nu(x,u).
$$

For each arm and stratum, let

$$
S_{a,r}
\triangleq
\mathcal L(V\mid A=a,R=r).
$$

### Lemma 2. Cross-view audit factorization

Under CV3,

$$
\boxed{S_{a,r}=\mathcal KQ_{a,r}}
$$

for every retained $(a,r)$. Consequently,

$$
S_{1,r}-S_{0,r}=\mathcal K\nu_r.
$$

**Proof.** Conditional on $(X,U,A,R)$, Lemma 1 says that $W^{\mathrm{audit}}$ follows the arm-invariant channel in the definition of $K$. Integrating this common conditional law against $Q_{a,r}$ gives the result. $\square$

For probability measures $P$ and $Q$ on the audit space, define

$$
\operatorname{MMD}_k(P,Q)
\triangleq
\left\|
\int k(v,\cdot)\,d(P-Q)(v)
\right\|_{\mathcal H_k}.
$$

The stratum-specific and aggregate audit discrepancies are

$$
\Delta_r(R)
\triangleq
\operatorname{MMD}_k(S_{1,r},S_{0,r}),
\qquad
D(R)
\triangleq
\sum_{r=1}^M p_r\Delta_r(R).
$$

Define the audit embedding operator

$$
J\nu
\triangleq
\int k(v,\cdot)\,d(\mathcal K\nu)(v).
$$

Lemma 2 immediately gives

$$
\Delta_r(R)=\|J\nu_r\|_{\mathcal H_k}.
$$

Because $k$ is characteristic,

$$
D(R)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
V\perp\!\!\!\perp A\mid R.
$$

This equivalence is observable at the population level. It does not by itself establish causal identification.

## 5. Exact identification

For each retained stratum $r$, prespecify a real linear space $\mathcal C_r$ of finite signed measures on $(X,U)$. Every element has total mass zero, the realized contrast $\nu_r$ belongs to $\mathcal C_r$, and

$$
\int |\mu_{a,r}(x,u)|\,d|\nu|(x,u)<\infty
$$

for every $a$ and $\nu\in\mathcal C_r$. Define the outcome functionals

$$
\ell_{a,r}(\nu)
\triangleq
\int\mu_{a,r}(x,u)\,d\nu(x,u).
$$

Sections 3 through 6 concern one fixed representation. Their notation suppresses dependence on the underlying map $\phi$. Section 8 restores all map and mask indices before making a uniform learning statement.

### CV5. Outcome-relevant audit null space

For each $a$, $r$, and $\nu\in\mathcal C_r$,

$$
J\nu=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\ell_{a,r}(\nu)=0.
$$

CV5 is weaker than recovery of the complete latent distribution. It requires only that the audit channel not erase a latent contrast that changes an outcome regression relevant to the target effect. The contrast spaces and the implication are prespecified restrictions on every full-data law in the declared model class. They cannot be chosen after inspecting the realized contrast or after selecting a favorable compatible full-data law.

When a candidate class is compared, restore the map index and require

$$
J\nu=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\ell_{a,\phi,r}(\nu)=0
\qquad
\text{for every }\nu\in\mathcal C_{\phi,r}
$$

simultaneously over all prespecified candidates, retained strata, and arms. In the cross-mask setting, these objects become $J_j$, $\mathcal C_{j,\phi,r}$, and $\ell_{a,j,\phi,r}$.

### Proposition 1. Conditional mixture injectivity is sufficient

Suppose the audit mixture operator is injective on $\mathcal C_r$:

$$
\mathcal K\nu=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\nu=0
\qquad
\text{for every }\nu\in\mathcal C_r.
$$

If the characteristic kernel embedding is injective on the image $\mathcal K(\mathcal C_r)$, then CV5 holds for every integrable outcome functional $\ell_{a,r}$.

**Proof.** If $J\nu=0$, injectivity of the kernel embedding gives $\mathcal K\nu=0$. Mixture injectivity then gives $\nu=0$, so every linear functional satisfies $\ell_{a,r}(\nu)=0$. $\square$

Injectivity is therefore an interpretable sufficient condition, not the logically minimal condition. The minimal exact condition is CV5.

### Theorem 2. Exact cross-view point identification

Assume CV1 through CV5. If the learned representation attains exact audit balance,

$$
D(R)=0,
$$

then

$$
\tau_R=\tau.
$$

Consequently the average treatment effect is point identified by the observed-data functional

$$
\boxed{
\tau
=
\sum_{r=1}^M p_r
\left[
\mathbb E(Y\mid A=1,R=r)
-
\mathbb E(Y\mid A=0,R=r)
\right].
}
$$

**Proof.** Exact balance gives $\Delta_r(R)=0$ for every positive-mass stratum. Hence $J\nu_r=0$. CV5 gives $\ell_{1,r}(\nu_r)=\ell_{0,r}(\nu_r)=0$. Substitute these equalities into Theorem 1. CV4 supplies the overlap needed to evaluate both observed outcome regressions in every retained stratum. $\square$

Exact observed balance alone is insufficient. Theorem 2 also needs the cross-view factorization from CV3 and the outcome-relevant null-space condition from CV5. Section 10 gives an explicit same-view counterexample.

## 6. Approximate balance and the sensitivity certificate

Exact zero is not expected in most finite samples or restricted representation classes. Quantitative control requires a stability condition that converts a small audit discrepancy into a small outcome-relevant latent contrast.

### CV6. Uniform audit stability

For each $a$, $r$, and $\nu\in\mathcal C_r$, there is a finite constant $\Gamma_{a,r}$ such that

$$
|\ell_{a,r}(\nu)|
\le
\Gamma_{a,r}\|J\nu\|_{\mathcal H_k}.
$$

For representation learning, restore the suppressed map index and write $\mathcal C_{\phi,r}$, $\mu_{a,\phi,r}$, $\ell_{a,\phi,r}$, and $\Gamma_{a,\phi,r}$. These objects and their integrability conditions must be prespecified for every candidate. The budget must hold uniformly over the representation class. We therefore require a declared common budget $\Gamma<\infty$ satisfying

$$
\Gamma_{a,\phi,r}\le\Gamma
$$

for every arm, retained stratum, and candidate representation under consideration, where

$$
|\ell_{a,\phi,r}(\nu)|
\le
\Gamma_{a,\phi,r}\|J\nu\|_{\mathcal H_k}
\qquad
\text{for every }\nu\in\mathcal C_{\phi,r}.
$$

The smallest valid stratum-specific constant is

$$
\Gamma_{a,r}^{\star}
\triangleq
\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal C_r:J\nu\ne0}
\frac{|\ell_{a,r}(\nu)|}{\|J\nu\|_{\mathcal H_k}},
$$

Use the following exhaustive conventions. If $J\nu=0$ but $\ell_{a,r}(\nu)\ne0$ for some admissible $\nu$, set $\Gamma_{a,r}^{\star}=\infty$. If $J(\mathcal C_r)=\{0\}$ and $\ell_{a,r}$ vanishes on $\mathcal C_r$, set $\Gamma_{a,r}^{\star}=0$.

### Proposition 2. Minimal stability and bounded factorization

The following statements are equivalent for fixed $(a,r)$:

1. $\Gamma_{a,r}^{\star}<\infty$.
2. The rule $J\nu\mapsto\ell_{a,r}(\nu)$ is a well-defined bounded linear functional on $J(\mathcal C_r)$.
3. There is a unique minimum-norm element

   $$
   g_{a,r}^{\star}
   \in
   \overline{J(\mathcal C_r)}
   $$

   such that

   $$
   \ell_{a,r}(\nu)
   =
   \langle g_{a,r}^{\star},J\nu\rangle_{\mathcal H_k}
   $$

   for every $\nu\in\mathcal C_r$.

When these statements hold,

$$
\Gamma_{a,r}^{\star}=\|g_{a,r}^{\star}\|_{\mathcal H_k}.
$$

**Proof.** Finiteness forces $\ell_{a,r}$ to vanish on the null space of $J$, so the displayed rule is well-defined. Its operator norm is the stated supremum. It extends continuously to $\overline{J(\mathcal C_r)}$, where the Riesz representation theorem gives the unique minimum-norm representer. The reverse direction follows from Cauchy-Schwarz. $\square$

One sufficient structural witness is an audit outcome bridge. Define

$$
(Tg)(x,u)
\triangleq
\mathbb E\{g(V)\mid X=x,U=u\}.
$$

If $\mu_{a,r}=Tg_{a,r}$ on the support of every admissible contrast and $\|g_{a,r}\|_{\mathcal H_k}\le\Gamma$, then

$$
\ell_{a,r}(\nu)
=
\langle g_{a,r},J\nu\rangle_{\mathcal H_k},
$$

so CV6 holds. The bridge is a structural witness. The proposed algorithm does not estimate it.

### Theorem 3. Population cross-view sensitivity certificate

Under CV1 through CV4 and CV6,

$$
|\tau_R-\tau|
\le
\sum_{r=1}^M p_r
\left[
(1-\pi_r)\Gamma_{1,r}
+
\pi_r\Gamma_{0,r}
\right]
\Delta_r(R).
$$

Under the common budget $\Gamma$,

$$
\boxed{
|\tau_R-\tau|
\le
\Gamma D(R).
}
$$

**Proof.** Apply the triangle inequality to Theorem 1. Lemma 2 identifies $\|J\nu_r\|$ with $\Delta_r(R)$, and CV6 bounds each outcome functional. The common-budget result follows from $(1-\pi_r)+\pi_r=1$. $\square$

The parameter $\Gamma$ is not generally identified from the observed single-proxy distribution. The scientifically honest output is therefore a sensitivity curve indexed by $\Gamma$, unless external information calibrates a credible range. A small $D(R)$ is not a causal guarantee without a common stability budget.

If the kernel is rescaled to $k_c=c^2k$ with $c>0$, then $D$ is multiplied by $c$ and the minimal stability constants are divided by $c$. The product $\Gamma D$ is unchanged. This invariance does not extend to bandwidth, kernel-family, or coordinate-weight changes.

## 7. Rotating cross-mask identification and robust aggregation

A high-dimensional proxy permits repeated held-out audits without declaring one coordinate permanently special. Write

$$
W=(W_1,\ldots,W_J),
$$

and, for each mask $j\in[J]$, define

$$
W_{-j}\triangleq(W_1,\ldots,W_{j-1},W_{j+1},\ldots,W_J),
$$

$$
Z_j\triangleq\phi_j(X,W_{-j}),
\qquad
V_j\triangleq(X,W_j).
$$

The coordinate $W_j$ is excluded from the input to $\phi_j$ and retained only for auditing mask $j$.

### All-mask observed regularity

Consistency, $Y=Y(A)$, remains a global assumption. For every mask $j$ and every positive-probability stratum $z$, assume

$$
0<P(A=1\mid Z_j=z)<1,
$$

and

$$
\mathbb E(|Y|\mid A=a,Z_j=z)<\infty,
\qquad a\in\{0,1\}.
$$

Also assume $\mathbb E|Y(a)|<\infty$. These observed regularity conditions apply to every mask, including causally invalid masks, because the median below is formed from all mask-specific functionals. Define

$$
\theta_j
\triangleq
\sum_{z:p_{j,z}>0}p_{j,z}
\left[
\mathbb E(Y\mid A=1,Z_j=z)
-
\mathbb E(Y\mid A=0,Z_j=z)
\right],
$$

where $p_{j,z}\triangleq P(Z_j=z)$.

For a fixed mask and stratum, define

$$
Q_{a,j,z}
\triangleq
\mathcal L(X,U\mid A=a,Z_j=z),
\qquad
\nu_{j,z}\triangleq Q_{1,j,z}-Q_{0,j,z},
$$

$$
\mu_{a,j,z}(x,u)
\triangleq
\mathbb E\{Y(a)\mid X=x,U=u,Z_j=z\},
$$

and

$$
\ell_{a,j,z}(\nu)
\triangleq
\int\mu_{a,j,z}(x,u)\,d\nu(x,u).
$$

Let $K_j(\cdot\mid x,u)$ be the conditional law of $W_j$ given $(X,U)=(x,u)$. It induces the audit mixture operator

$$
(\mathcal K_j\nu)(C)
\triangleq
\int\!\int
\mathbf 1\{(x,w_j)\in C\}
K_j(dw_j\mid x,u)\,\nu(dx,du).
$$

For a characteristic kernel $k_j$ on the space of $V_j$, define

$$
J_j\nu
\triangleq
\int k_j(v,\cdot)\,d(\mathcal K_j\nu)(v).
$$

Let $D_j(Z_j)$ be the aggregate held-out discrepancy obtained by applying the definition of $D$ in Section 4 to $V_j$ within strata of $Z_j$.

### Definition 1. Exact-valid mask

Before observing the data, fix a linear class $\mathcal C_{j,z}$ of finite signed, total-mass-zero contrasts for every $(j,z)$. Require

$$
\int|\mu_{a,j,z}|\,d|\nu|<\infty
$$

for every arm, retained stratum, and $\nu\in\mathcal C_{j,z}$. This integrability requirement is inherited by both the exact-valid and stable-valid definitions. A mask $j$ is **exact-valid** if all five conditions below hold.

1. For every $a\in\{0,1\}$,

   $$
   A\perp\!\!\!\perp\bigl(Y(a),W_{-j}\bigr)\mid(X,U).
   $$

2. The held-out view satisfies

   $$
   W_j\perp\!\!\!\perp(A,W_{-j})\mid(X,U).
   $$

3. Every realized contrast satisfies $\nu_{j,z}\in\mathcal C_{j,z}$.

4. The class-wide outcome-null-space implication holds:

   $$
   J_j\nu=0
   \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
   \ell_{a,j,z}(\nu)=0
   $$

   for every arm, retained stratum, and $\nu\in\mathcal C_{j,z}$.

5. Exact observed audit balance holds: $D_j(Z_j)=0$.

Write $\mathcal V_0$ for the unknown set of exact-valid masks. This definition does not use the desired conclusion $\theta_j=\tau$.

### Theorem 4. Mask-specific exact identification

Under consistency and all-mask observed regularity, every $j\in\mathcal V_0$ satisfies

$$
\boxed{\theta_j=\tau.}
$$

**Proof.** The first structural condition and the fact that $Z_j$ is a measurable function of $(X,W_{-j})$ give the representation-safe exchangeability used in Theorem 1. The second structural condition gives a common audit channel after conditioning on $Z_j$, so the cross-view factorization yields

$$
\|J_j\nu_{j,z}\|_{\mathcal H_{k_j}}=\Delta_{j,z}.
$$

Exact balance makes every $\Delta_{j,z}$ zero. Contrast membership and the class-wide null-space implication therefore make both outcome functionals vanish. Substitution into the exact bias identity gives $\theta_j-\tau=0$. $\square$

For ordered real numbers $t_{(1)}\le\cdots\le t_{(J)}$, define the conventional median, including the even-$J$ case, by

$$
\operatorname{Med}_J(t_1,\ldots,t_J)
\triangleq
\frac{t_{(\lceil J/2\rceil)}+t_{(\lfloor J/2\rfloor+1)}}2.
$$

### Lemma 3. Strict-majority median robustness

If more than half of $t_1,\ldots,t_J$ lie in $[c-b,c+b]$, then

$$
\left|\operatorname{Med}_J(t_1,\ldots,t_J)-c\right|\le b.
$$

**Proof.** At most $\lceil J/2\rceil-1$ values lie below $c-b$, and at most that many lie above $c+b$. Both central order statistics are therefore in $[c-b,c+b]$, as is their average. $\square$

### Assumption 7. Strict-majority exact validity

The fixed population maps satisfy

$$
|\mathcal V_0|>\frac J2.
$$

### Theorem 5. Cross-mask median point identification

Under consistency, all-mask observed regularity, and Assumption 7,

$$
\boxed{
\tau
=
\operatorname{Med}_J(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_J).
}
$$

**Proof.** Theorem 4 gives $\theta_j=\tau$ for every $j\in\mathcal V_0$. A strict majority of the mask-specific functionals is therefore equal to $\tau$. Apply Lemma 3 with $b=0$. $\square$

The majority condition is structural. It is not testable from the observed discrepancy, is not implied by high dimension, and is not guaranteed by the learning algorithm.

### Theorem 6. Majority-stable approximate certificate

Let $\mathcal V_\Gamma$ contain masks satisfying the first three exact-validity conditions and, for a prespecified $\Gamma_j<\infty$,

$$
|\ell_{a,j,z}(\nu)|
\le
\Gamma_j\|J_j\nu\|_{\mathcal H_{k_j}}
$$

for every arm, retained stratum, and $\nu\in\mathcal C_{j,z}$. Exact balance is not required. If $|\mathcal V_\Gamma|>J/2$, then

$$
\boxed{
\left|
\operatorname{Med}_J(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_J)-\tau
\right|
\le
\max_{j\in\mathcal V_\Gamma}
\Gamma_jD_j(Z_j).
}
$$

**Proof.** Theorem 3 gives $|\theta_j-\tau|\le\Gamma_jD_j(Z_j)$ for every stable-valid mask. A strict majority therefore lies in the interval centered at $\tau$ with radius $\max_{j\in\mathcal V_\Gamma}\Gamma_jD_j(Z_j)$. Lemma 3 completes the proof. $\square$

### Proposition 3. Invalid-mask-robust interval aggregation

Let $I_j=[L_j,U_j]$ be mask-specific intervals, and suppose at most $b<J/2$ masks are invalid. If the intervals for all valid masks cover $\tau$ simultaneously with probability at least $1-\alpha$, then, for endpoint order statistics,

$$
I_{\mathrm{agg}}^{(b)}
\triangleq
[L_{(J-b)},U_{(b+1)}]
$$

covers $\tau$ with probability at least $1-\alpha$. The set is declared empty if the lower endpoint exceeds the upper endpoint.

**Proof.** On the simultaneous coverage event, at least $J-b$ lower endpoints are at most $\tau$, so $L_{(J-b)}\le\tau$. At most $b$ upper endpoints are below $\tau$, so $U_{(b+1)}\ge\tau$. $\square$

Marginal mask-wise coverage is insufficient. The probability statement requires simultaneous coverage over the unknown valid set.

### Proposition 4. One unknown valid mask does not identify a scalar

Fix $\boldsymbol\theta=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_J)$. In the abstract contamination model whose only restriction is that $\theta_j=\tau$ for at least one unknown index, the identified set is

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal I_1(\boldsymbol\theta)
=
\{\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_J\}.
}
$$

This is an abstract aggregation boundary, not a causal observational-equivalence theorem. One unknown valid mask supports a union of candidates, not point identification by their median.

### Proposition 5. A primitive redundant-measurement route

Suppose the full conditional law factorizes as

$$
\begin{aligned}
&P\{dA,dY(0),dY(1),dw_1,\ldots,dw_J\mid X,U\}\\
&\quad=
P(dA\mid X,U)
P\{dY(0),dY(1)\mid X,U\}
\prod_{\ell=1}^J K_\ell(dw_\ell\mid X,U).
\end{aligned}
$$

Let $\mathcal G\subset[J]$ satisfy $|\mathcal G|>J/2$. For every $j\in\mathcal G$ and retained $z$, require the realized contrast membership $\nu_{j,z}\in\mathcal C_{j,z}$ and conditional mixture injectivity on the same prespecified contrast class:

$$
\mathcal K_j\nu=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\nu=0,
\qquad
\nu\in\mathcal C_{j,z}.
$$

Then the two cross-view independence conditions hold for every $j\in\mathcal G$, and characteristic kernel embedding plus mixture-operator injectivity implies the class-wide outcome-null-space condition. If there is a set $\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal G$ with $|\mathcal H|>J/2$ such that exact audit balance and observed regularity hold for every $j\in\mathcal H$, Theorem 5 identifies $\tau$.

**Proof.** Marginalizing the displayed product law gives both cross-view independences. If $J_j\nu=0$, write the zero-mass signed measure $\mathcal K_j\nu$ by its Jordan decomposition as $c(P-Q)$. Characteristicness gives $P=Q$, hence $\mathcal K_j\nu=0$. Mixture-operator injectivity then gives $\nu=0$, so every integrable outcome functional vanishes. Exact balance and overlap remain separate requirements. $\square$

The factorization and injectivity do not imply exact balance, representation overlap, or learning success. They supply a transparent sufficient route to the structural part of mask validity.

### All-mask-valid average benchmark

If every mask satisfies Theorem 3, then the arithmetic mean obeys

$$
\left|
\frac1J\sum_{j=1}^J\theta_j-\tau
\right|
\le
\frac1J\sum_{j=1}^J\Gamma_jD_j(Z_j).
$$

This benchmark is efficient to analyze when all masks are valid, but it is not robust to even one arbitrarily invalid mask.

## 8. Finite hard-strata learning

This section gives explicit learning guarantees for finite classes of hard-strata maps. Neural networks, random-feature approximations, and soft assignments are addressed in the status ledger rather than being covered by this theorem.

For each mask $j$, let $\Phi_j$ be a finite class of maps from $(X,W_{-j})$ to $[M_j]$. For $\phi\in\Phi_j$, write

$$
Z_j^\phi\triangleq\phi(X,W_{-j}).
$$

For the concentration result only, assume that every positive-mass candidate stratum satisfies

$$
p_{j,r}(\phi)
\triangleq
P(Z_j^\phi=r)
\ge p_{\min},
$$

and

$$
\eta
\le
P(A=1\mid Z_j^\phi=r)
\le
1-\eta.
$$

No causal stability condition is needed for concentration. Set

$$
M\triangleq\max_jM_j,
\qquad
N_\Phi\triangleq\sum_{j=1}^J|\Phi_j|,
\qquad
\kappa\triangleq\max_j\kappa_j,
$$

where $\kappa_j$ bounds the diagonal of the kernel used for audit view $V_j$.

For a candidate $\phi\in\Phi_j$, let

$$
\mathcal R_j(\phi)
\triangleq
\{r:P(Z_j^\phi=r)>0\}.
$$

Population and empirical sums exclude labels outside $\mathcal R_j(\phi)$. Such labels have zero empirical count almost surely. Let $n_{a,r}^{j}(\phi)$ be the number of sample observations with $A=a$ and $Z_j^\phi=r$. Let $\widehat p_r^j(\phi)$ be the empirical stratum mass, and let $\widehat m_{a,r}^j(\phi)$ be the empirical kernel mean of $V_j$ in that cell. On the count event used below, every retained $(a,r)$ empirical cell is nonempty. Define

$$
\widehat\Delta_r^j(\phi)
\triangleq
\|\widehat m_{1,r}^j(\phi)-\widehat m_{0,r}^j(\phi)\|_{\mathcal H_{k_j}},
$$

$$
\widehat D_j(\phi)
\triangleq
\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_j(\phi)}\widehat p_r^j(\phi)\widehat\Delta_r^j(\phi).
$$

### Theorem 7. Simultaneous finite-class deviation

Under the uniform candidate mass and overlap conditions stated above, let $\delta\in(0,1)$ and suppose

$$
n
\ge
\frac{8}{p_{\min}\eta}
\log\frac{8MN_\Phi}{\delta}.
$$

Define

$$
e_n(\delta)
\triangleq
\left(
2\kappa
+
\kappa\sqrt{2\log\frac{4MN_\Phi}{\delta}}
\right)
\sqrt{\frac{2}{np_{\min}\eta}},
$$

and

$$
r_n(\delta)
\triangleq
2e_n(\delta)
+
2\kappa
\sqrt{
\frac{2\left\{M\log2+\log\frac{4N_\Phi}{\delta}\right\}}{n}
}.
$$

Then, with probability at least $1-\delta$,

$$
\boxed{
\sup_{1\le j\le J}
\sup_{\phi\in\Phi_j}
|\widehat D_j(\phi)-D_j(\phi)|
\le
r_n(\delta).
}
$$

**Proof.** Allocate the failure probability as $\delta/4+\delta/2+\delta/4$. First, a multiplicative Chernoff bound and a union bound over at most $2MN_\Phi$ retained empirical cells give, with failure probability at most $\delta/4$, the count event

$$
n_{a,r}^j(\phi)
\ge
\frac12np_{\min}\eta
$$

simultaneously over all mask-map-arm-stratum cells. Second, conditional on the cell assignments for one fixed map, the observations in each retained cell are i.i.d. from the corresponding audit law. Bounded-kernel mean-embedding concentration, followed by a union bound over the cells with total budget $\delta/2$, gives an embedding error no larger than $e_n(\delta)$, hence a discrepancy error no larger than $2e_n(\delta)$. Third, the multinomial $L_1$ bound and a union bound over the $N_\Phi$ candidates, with total budget $\delta/4$, control the empirical stratum weights by the second term in $r_n(\delta)$. On the intersection of the three events, the triangle inequality gives the displayed uniform bound. The three failure budgets sum to $\delta$. $\square$

For each mask, suppose the learning algorithm returns an approximate empirical minimizer satisfying

$$
\widehat D_j(\widehat\phi_j)
\le
\inf_{\phi\in\Phi_j}\widehat D_j(\phi)
+
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt},j}.
$$

For the causal part of the oracle inequality, now add the following assumptions. For every mask $j$ and treatment level $a$,

$$
A\perp\!\!\!\perp\bigl(Y(a),W_{-j}\bigr)\mid(X,U),
\qquad
W_j\perp\!\!\!\perp(A,W_{-j})\mid(X,U).
$$

For every $(j,\phi,r)$ with $\phi\in\Phi_j$ and $r\in\mathcal R_j(\phi)$, define

$$
Q_{a,j,\phi,r}
\triangleq
\mathcal L(X,U\mid A=a,Z_j^\phi=r),
\qquad
\nu_{j,\phi,r}
\triangleq
Q_{1,j,\phi,r}-Q_{0,j,\phi,r},
$$

$$
\mu_{a,j,\phi,r}(x,u)
\triangleq
\mathbb E\{Y(a)\mid X=x,U=u,Z_j^\phi=r\}.
$$

Prespecify $\mathcal C_{j,\phi,r}$ containing $\nu_{j,\phi,r}$, require $\int|\mu_{a,j,\phi,r}|\,d|\nu|<\infty$, and define

$$
\ell_{a,j,\phi,r}(\nu)
\triangleq
\int\mu_{a,j,\phi,r}\,d\nu.
$$

Let $J_j$ be the held-out audit embedding operator. Assume a common budget $\Gamma_j<\infty$ satisfies

$$
|\ell_{a,j,\phi,r}(\nu)|
\le
\Gamma_j\|J_j\nu\|_{\mathcal H_{k_j}}
$$

uniformly over $\phi$, retained $r$, $a$, and $\nu\in\mathcal C_{j,\phi,r}$.

### Corollary 1. Majority-robust cross-mask ERM oracle inequality

On the event of Theorem 7,

$$
D_j(\widehat\phi_j)
\le
\inf_{\phi\in\Phi_j}D_j(\phi)
+
2r_n(\delta)
+
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt},j}
$$

simultaneously for every mask. Suppose the selected maps have a strict-majority stable-valid set $\widehat{\mathcal V}_\Gamma$. Let $\theta_{j,\widehat\phi_j}$ denote the population adjustment functional induced by $Z_j^{\widehat\phi_j}$. Then

$$
\left|
\operatorname{Med}_J
\left(
\theta_{1,\widehat\phi_1},\ldots,
\theta_{J,\widehat\phi_J}
\right)
-\tau
\right|
\le
\max_{j\in\widehat{\mathcal V}_\Gamma}
\Gamma_j
\left{
\inf_{\phi\in\Phi_j}D_j(\phi)
+2r_n(\delta)
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt},j}
\right}.
$$

**Proof.** The deterministic three-term ERM comparison gives the first inequality. Apply Theorem 6 to the selected maps and substitute those bounds. $\square$

If every selected mask is stable-valid, the arithmetic average retains the all-mask-valid benchmark obtained by averaging the same mask-specific bounds. The median result requires only a strict majority.

The oracle inequality compares certified worst-case bias, not necessarily realized bias. The two quantities coincide only under additional alignment conditions that are not part of the final cross-view theory.

## 9. Fixed-representation estimation and honest evaluation

Fix one finite representation $Z$ independently of the sample used for estimation. Define

$$
m_a(z)\triangleq\mathbb E(Y\mid A=a,Z=z),
\qquad
e(z)\triangleq P(A=1\mid Z=z).
$$

The efficient influence function of the observed adjustment functional $\theta_Z$ in the nonparametric model for $(Z,A,Y)$ is

$$
\psi_Z(O)
\triangleq
\frac{A\{Y-m_1(Z)\}}{e(Z)}
-
\frac{(1-A)\{Y-m_0(Z)\}}{1-e(Z)}
+m_1(Z)-m_0(Z)-\theta_Z.
$$

The AIPW estimator is

$$
\widehat\theta_Z^{\mathrm{AIPW}}
\triangleq
\mathbb P_n
\left[
\widehat m_1(Z)-\widehat m_0(Z)
+
\frac{A\{Y-\widehat m_1(Z)\}}{\widehat e(Z)}
-
\frac{(1-A)\{Y-\widehat m_0(Z)\}}{1-\widehat e(Z)}
\right].
$$

When the nuisances are empirical cell means and every retained $(a,r)$ cell is nonempty, the residual terms vanish within cells. The estimator then equals the stratified plug-in estimator

$$
\widehat\theta_Z
=
\sum_{r=1}^M\widehat p_r
(\overline Y_{1,r}-\overline Y_{0,r}).
$$

For fixed finite $Z$, $\mathbb E[Y^2]<\infty$, and strict overlap,

$$
\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_Z-\theta_Z)
\rightsquigarrow
\mathcal N\{0,\mathbb E(\psi_Z^2)\},
$$

and the empirical-cell estimator is asymptotically efficient for $\theta_Z$.

For fixed representations $Z_1,\ldots,Z_J$, the all-mask-valid average benchmark has efficient influence function

$$
\overline\psi(O)
\triangleq
\frac1J\sum_{j=1}^J\psi_{Z_j}(O),
$$

and its estimator is the corresponding average

$$
\widehat{\overline\theta}_Z
\triangleq
\frac1J\sum_{j=1}^J\widehat\theta_{Z_j}.
$$

The variance $\mathbb E(\overline\psi^2)$ automatically includes dependence among masks. This average result requires all masks to be part of the target benchmark. It is not an influence function for the median. These efficiency statements concern prespecified fixed representations and do not establish same-sample inference after adaptive neural representation learning.

### Honest sample-splitting procedure

1. Use a training fold to learn $\widehat\phi_1,\ldots,\widehat\phi_J$ without using outcomes.
2. Freeze all learned maps, kernels, bandwidths, and masks.
3. On an independent evaluation fold, compute each held-out audit discrepancy $\widehat D_j(\widehat\phi_j)$ and empirical overlap diagnostics.
4. On that evaluation fold, estimate each $\theta_j$ and take their conventional median.
5. Report the point estimate together with a sensitivity curve indexed by the declared budgets $\Gamma_j$.

### Theorem 8. Consistency after representation learning

Let $\widehat{\mathcal V}_{0,n}$ be the random exact-valid set induced by the training fold. Suppose

$$
\mathcal E_n
\triangleq
\left\{
|\widehat{\mathcal V}_{0,n}|>\frac J2
\text{, every population mask functional is well-defined, and }
\widehat\theta_{j,n}\in\mathbb R\text{ for every }j
\right\},
\qquad
P(\mathcal E_n)\longrightarrow1,
$$

and, on this event,

$$
\max_{j\in\widehat{\mathcal V}_{0,n}}
|\widehat\theta_{j,n}-\theta_{j,n}|
\xrightarrow{p}0.
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Med}_J
(\widehat\theta_{1,n},\ldots,\widehat\theta_{J,n})
\xrightarrow{p}\tau.
}
$$

**Proof.** Conditional on the training fold, the maps are fixed. Theorem 4 makes every valid population functional equal to $\tau$. On $\mathcal E_n$, Lemma 3 bounds the median error by the largest estimation error over the valid masks. Both $P(\mathcal E_n^c)$ and that largest error vanish. $\square$

No independence among mask-specific estimators is required. Majority validity is structural and is not learned merely by minimizing audit discrepancy.

Empirical overlap diagnostics do not prove population overlap. The finite-sample theorem below assumes the per-mask joint causal and audit conditions explicitly. It also assumes that, for almost every training-sample realization, the selected maps satisfy the population mass, overlap, integrability, and uniform stability conditions.

### Theorem 9. All-mask-valid average held-out certificate

Let the independent evaluation fold have size $m$, and suppose $|Y|\le B$. For every mask $j$ and treatment level $a$, assume

$$
A\perp\!\!\!\perp\bigl(Y(a),W_{-j}\bigr)\mid(X,U),
\qquad
W_j\perp\!\!\!\perp(A,W_{-j})\mid(X,U).
$$

Assume that, for almost every training-fold realization, every selected representation has at most $M$ retained strata and satisfies the population mass and overlap bounds with the same $p_{\min}$ and $\eta$. For each selected map, require a prespecified contrast space that contains every realized stratum contrast, makes its $\ell_{a,j,\widehat\phi_j,r}$ integrable, and satisfies

$$
|\ell_{a,j,\widehat\phi_j,r}(\nu)|
\le
\Gamma_j\|J_j\nu\|_{\mathcal H_{k_j}}
$$

uniformly over arms, retained strata, and admissible contrasts. Let every audit kernel have diagonal bounded by $\kappa^2$.

For $\delta_D,\delta_Y\in(0,1)$, suppose

$$
m\ge
\frac{8}{p_{\min}\eta}
\max\left\{
\log\frac{8MJ}{\delta_D},
\log\frac{16MJ}{\delta_Y}
\right\}.
$$

Define

$$
e_m^{\mathrm{eval}}(\delta_D)
\triangleq
\left(
2\kappa
+
\kappa\sqrt{2\log\frac{4MJ}{\delta_D}}
\right)
\sqrt{\frac{2}{mp_{\min}\eta}},
$$

$$
r_m^{\mathrm{eval}}(\delta_D)
\triangleq
2e_m^{\mathrm{eval}}(\delta_D)
+
2\kappa
\sqrt{
\frac{2\left\{M\log2+\log\frac{4J}{\delta_D}\right\}}{m}
},
$$

and

$$
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{est}}(\delta_Y)
\triangleq
2B\sqrt{
\frac{4\log\frac{8MJ}{\delta_Y}}{mp_{\min}\eta}
}
+
2B\sqrt{
\frac{2\left\{M\log2+\log\frac{4J}{\delta_Y}\right\}}{m}
}.
$$

Conditionally on the training fold, with probability at least $1-\delta_D-\delta_Y$,

$$
\boxed{
|\widehat{\overline\theta}_Z-\tau|
\le
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{est}}(\delta_Y)
+
\frac1J\sum_{j=1}^J
\Gamma_j
\left{
\widehat D_j(\widehat\phi_j)
+r_m^{\mathrm{eval}}(\delta_D)
\right}.
}
$$

**Proof.** Conditional on the training fold, all selected maps are fixed. Apply the single-map kernel-embedding argument simultaneously over the $J$ masks to obtain

$$
D_j(\widehat\phi_j)
\le
\widehat D_j(\widehat\phi_j)
+r_m^{\mathrm{eval}}(\delta_D).
$$

The count, conditional Hoeffding, and multinomial weight bounds applied simultaneously over masks give

$$
\left|
\widehat{\overline\theta}_Z
-
\frac1J\sum_j\theta_j
\right|
\le
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{est}}(\delta_Y).
$$

Apply Theorem 3 mask by mask, average the resulting bounds as in the all-mask-valid benchmark, and use a union bound. $\square$

If the finite-class training event in Theorem 7 also holds, the same argument gives the oracle form

$$
|\widehat{\overline\theta}_Z-\tau|
\le
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{est}}(\delta_Y)
+
\frac1J\sum_{j=1}^J
\Gamma_j
\left{
\inf_{\phi\in\Phi_j}D_j(\phi)
+2r_n(\delta)
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt},j}
\right}
$$

on the intersection of the stated training and evaluation events.

### Theorem 10. End-to-end held-out median certificate

Under the same evaluation-sample size, mass, overlap, boundedness, and kernel conditions, suppose the selected maps have a stable-valid set $\widehat{\mathcal V}_{\Gamma}$ satisfying

$$
|\widehat{\mathcal V}_{\Gamma}|>\frac J2,
$$

and every mask-specific observed functional is finite and well-defined. The structural and stability requirements need hold for the masks in $\widehat{\mathcal V}_{\Gamma}$, not for every mask. Conditionally on the training fold, with probability at least $1-\delta_D-\delta_Y$,

$$
\boxed{
\left|
\operatorname{Med}_J(\widehat\theta_1,\ldots,\widehat\theta_J)
-\tau
\right|
\le
\max_{j\in\widehat{\mathcal V}_{\Gamma}}
\left[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{est}}(\delta_Y)
+\Gamma_j
\left\{
\widehat D_j(\widehat\phi_j)
+r_m^{\mathrm{eval}}(\delta_D)
\right\}
\right].
}
$$

**Proof.** Conditional on training, simultaneous kernel concentration bounds every stable-valid population discrepancy by its empirical discrepancy plus $r_m^{\mathrm{eval}}$. The count, conditional Hoeffding, and multinomial bounds control every mask-specific estimation error by $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{est}}$. Theorem 6 controls each stable-valid mask, and Lemma 3 aggregates the strict majority. $\square$

If mask-specific sensitivity intervals cover $\tau$ simultaneously for all valid masks, Proposition 3 gives the invalid-mask-robust aggregate interval. Marginal mask-wise coverage is insufficient.

The median map is generally nondifferentiable when a strict majority of population mask functionals tie at $\tau$. A regular median influence function, semiparametric efficiency claim, and ordinary Gaussian limit therefore remain open. The fixed-mask AIPW result and the all-mask average benchmark do not establish them.

## 10. Adversarial conditioning audit

This section records why the same proxy coordinates cannot both define and certify a representation without an additional residual-information argument.

### Proposition 6. Exact same-view balance does not identify the effect

Let $X$ be absent and

$$
U\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2),
$$

$$
P(A=1\mid U=1)=0.8,
\qquad
P(A=1\mid U=0)=0.2.
$$

Let

$$
W=U\oplus E,
\qquad
E\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(0.2),
$$

with $E\perp\!\!\!\perp(A,U)$, and let

$$
Y(a)=\tau a+\beta U+\varepsilon,
\qquad
\varepsilon\perp\!\!\!\perp(A,U,W).
$$

Choose the same-view representation

$$
R=P(A=1\mid W).
$$

Then $R$ is one-to-one with $W$, so

$$
W\perp\!\!\!\perp A\mid R
$$

holds exactly. Nevertheless,

$$
P(U=1\mid A=1,W=1)=\frac{16}{17},
\qquad
P(U=1\mid A=0,W=1)=\frac12,
$$

and adjustment by $R$ has bias

$$
\tau_R-\tau=\frac{15}{34}\beta.
$$

**Proof.** The propensity values are $P(A=1\mid W=1)=0.68$ and $P(A=1\mid W=0)=0.32$, so $R$ and $W$ generate the same sigma-field. Exact balance is therefore automatic. Bayes' rule gives the two displayed latent probabilities, and direct standardization over the two $W$ strata gives the stated bias. $\square$

This counterexample is not a failure of the cross-view theorem. It violates the construction because the audited variable $W$ is also copied into $R$. Holding out $W^{\mathrm{audit}}$ keeps the channel in Lemma 2 from degenerating for this purely representational reason.

## 11. Testability, implementation gaps, and theorem-status ledger

The following distinction is part of the method rather than a qualification added after the fact.

- Population audit balance $V_j\perp\!\!\!\perp A\mid Z_j$ and representation overlap concern the observed law. Finite data can diagnose them but cannot prove exact equality.
- CV2 and CV3 are full-data structural restrictions. They are not generally testable from the observed law alone.
- CV5 and CV6 constrain unobserved latent contrasts. They are not identified from a generic single-proxy distribution.
- The stability budgets $\Gamma_j$ must therefore be reported as sensitivity parameters unless external validation supplies calibration.
- Splitting one high-dimensional $W$ into views is scientifically credible only when the chosen coordinates or blocks plausibly satisfy CV3. Cycling over masks does not make CV3 automatic.
